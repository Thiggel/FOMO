\begin{abstract}
Self-supervised learning (SSL) has allowed for advancements in language processing and computer vision, as unlabelled data is available in large quantities. However, imbalances in training datasets can lead to strong biases in the learned features of pre-trained models. Previous results show that pre-training using imbalanced data can also hurt downstream performance. We propose a data-centric approach: our method trains on the data, finds underrepresented samples, and uses diffusion to generate novel data complementing the underrepresented images. Our proposed method, BRIDGE (Balancing Representations by Identifying and Generating Under-represented Data), utilizes distance-based outlier detection to identify regions of the embedding space that are underrepresented in each training cycle. Experimental results on ImageNet-100-LT demonstrate that BRIDGE consistently outperforms both balanced and imbalanced baselines, with significant improvements on fine-grained classification tasks. Detailed ablation studies confirm that both out-of-distribution sample selection and diffusion-based generation contribute substantially to the effectiveness of our approach, offering a promising alternative to model-centric solutions for addressing imbalance in self-supervised learning.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Self-supervised learning (SSL) methods have emerged as powerful techniques for learning transferable features across diverse tasks~\citep{chen2020simple, DINO, oquab2023dinov2}. However, their performance is significantly affected by dataset imbalance~\citep{assran2210hidden, tian2021divide, ts}. For instance, SimCLR underperforms on long-tailed datasets due to insufficient negative samples~\citep{tian2021divide}, while joint-embedding methods like VICReg assume uniform clustering, hampering performance on imbalanced data~\citep{assran2210hidden}.

Existing solutions typically adopt model-centric approaches, such as ensemble learning~\citep{tian2021divide}, incorporating arbitrary feature priors~\citep{assran2210hidden}, or modifying training dynamics~\citep{ts}. These approaches, however, often require prior knowledge of dataset distributions or extensive hyperparameter tuning, contradicting the unsupervised nature of SSL.

We propose BRIDGE (\textbf{B}alancing \textbf{R}epresentations with \textbf{A}nomaly \textbf{D}etection and \textbf{D}iffusion), a data-centric approach to address imbalance in SSL. BRIDGE divides training into cycles, identifies underrepresented samples via OOD detection after each cycle, and augments them using diffusion models for subsequent training. This approach (1) eliminates the need for prior dataset knowledge and (2) dynamically balances the latent space to avoid suboptimal local minima.

Experiments on ImageNet-100-LT across multiple architectures (ResNet-50, ViT-S, ViT-B) and SSL methods (SimCLR, DINO, MoCo) demonstrate that BRIDGE consistently outperforms both balanced and imbalanced baselines on diverse downstream tasks. BRIDGE achieves significant improvements on fine-grained tasks (up to 11.7\% on Oxford Flowers) and surpasses state-of-the-art methods on CIFAR-10 (73.3\%) and CIFAR-100 (45.4\%), showing that our data-centric approach offers a promising alternative to model-centric solutions.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related-work}

\noindent
\textbf{Self-Supervised Learning (SSL).} 
SSL has emerged as a powerful paradigm for representation learning without human-annotated labels. In computer vision, three main families of methods are widely adopted: (1) \emph{masked prediction}, where missing patches are reconstructed \citep{he2022masked}; (2) \emph{contrastive learning}, including MoCo \citep{he2020momentum} and SimCLR \citep{chen2020simple}; and (3) \emph{self-distillation}, exemplified by DINO \citep{DINO}. These approaches scale to large datasets and enable emergent abilities in pretrained models \citep{wei2022emergent}.

\noindent
\textbf{Imbalanced Representation Learning.} 
Imbalanced datasets bias representations toward majority classes and lead to degraded downstream transfer \citep{johnson2019survey}. Although SSL can be more resilient than supervised learning \citep{liu2021self}, recent studies reveal clear performance drops under long-tailed distributions \citep{assran2210hidden, tian2021divide}. Prior solutions are primarily \emph{model- or training-centric}. For example, Temperature Schedules (TS) \citep{ts} modulate the contrastive temperature across training to balance instance- and group-level discrimination, while Self-Damaging Contrastive Learning (SDCLR) \citep{sdclr} creates a pruned ``self-competitor'' network to emphasize hard and tail samples. While effective, these methods modify optimization dynamics and require careful hyperparameter tuning. In contrast, our work adopts a \emph{data-centric} perspective, repairing imbalance directly in the training distribution.

\noindent
\textbf{Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Detection.} 
OOD detection identifies samples that deviate from the bulk of the distribution and is crucial for dataset curation \citep{whang2023data}. Distance-based approaches \citep{sun2022out} measure how isolated an embedding is relative to its neighbors. These non-parametric methods avoid distributional assumptions, making them suitable for SSL on imbalanced, unlabeled data.

\noindent
\textbf{Diffusion-Based Image Generation.} 
Diffusion models generate high-quality images by denoising random noise \citep{ho2020denoising}. Stable Diffusion \citep{rombach2022highresolution}, particularly Stable Diffusion 2 UnCLIP, conditions on CLIP embeddings to produce semantically faithful variations. Its ability to synthesize diverse yet consistent images makes it a natural tool for augmenting underrepresented regions of the SSL feature space.



\section{Method: Balancing Representations by Identifying and Generating Underrepresented Data (BRIDGE)}
\label{sec:method}

We present \textbf{BRIDGE} (\textit{Balancing Representations by Identifying and Generating Underrepresented Data}), a data–centric framework designed to mitigate imbalance in self–supervised learning. A schematic of the method is shown in Figure \ref{fig:full_pipeline}. The central idea of BRIDGE is to alternate between learning representations on the current dataset and repairing the dataset by explicitly augmenting the regions of the embedding space that are least represented. This cyclic interaction between representation learning and dataset repair produces a training set that gradually fills the sparse areas of the feature space and leads to improved downstream transfer. 

\subsection{Problem Setting}

Let $D^{(0)}=\{x_i\}_{i=1}^{N_0}$ be an unlabeled, imbalanced dataset. We consider an encoder $f_{\theta}$ trained with a self–supervised objective $a$ such as SimCLR or DINO. Our method introduces a frozen generative model $g_{\phi}$, instantiated as Stable Diffusion 2 UnCLIP, which can generate semantically related images conditioned on a given input. Training is organized into $N_C$ \emph{cycles}, each consisting of a representation learning phase followed by a dataset repair phase. After cycle $c$, the dataset is denoted $D^{(c)}$ and the encoder has parameters $\theta^{(c)}$.

\subsection{Cyclic Training Procedure}

Each cycle contains two phases:

\paragraph{Phase 1: Representation learning.}
The encoder $f_{\theta}$ is trained with objective $a$ on the dataset $D^{(c)}$ for $N_E$ epochs. After training, we compute embeddings 
\[
z_i = f_{\theta}(x_i), \quad x_i \in D^{(c)}.
\]
These embeddings define the geometry of the current feature space.

\paragraph{Phase 2: Dataset repair.}
We identify the $N_{\text{OOD}}$ samples with the largest $k$–nearest neighbor distances in the embedding space, denoting them as $O^{(c)}$. For each such sample $x$, we generate $N_{\text{Aug}}$ variants using the frozen generator $g_{\phi}$:
\[
\{\hat{x}_{x,1}, \ldots, \hat{x}_{x,N_{\text{Aug}}}\} = g_{\phi}(x).
\]
The augmented dataset is then
\[
D^{(c+1)} = D^{(c)} \cup \{\hat{x}_{x,j} \mid x \in O^{(c)}, \, j=1,\ldots,N_{\text{Aug}}\}.
\]
The next cycle resumes training on $D^{(c+1)}$. Algorithm~\ref{alg:bridge} provides a summary.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{BRIDGE: Cyclic Self–Supervised Training with Dataset Repair}
\label{alg:bridge}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE \textbf{Input:} encoder $f_{\theta}$, SSL objective $a$, frozen generator $g_{\phi}$, cycles $N_C$, epochs per cycle $N_E$, dataset $D^{(0)}$, number of OOD samples $N_{\text{OOD}}$, generations per sample $N_{\text{Aug}}$, k for kNN
\FOR{$c=0$ to $N_C-1$}
    \STATE Train $f_{\theta}$ with $a$ on $D^{(c)}$ for $N_E$ epochs
    \STATE Compute embeddings $z_i=f_{\theta}(x_i)$ for all $x_i \in D^{(c)}$
    \STATE Compute kNN OOD scores ($k=100$) for each embedding
    \STATE Select $O^{(c)}$: top $N_{\text{OOD}}$ samples by score
    \FOR{each $x \in O^{(c)}$}
        \FOR{$j=1$ to $N_{\text{Aug}}$}
            \STATE $\hat{x}_{x,j} = g_{\phi}(x)$
            \STATE Add $\hat{x}_{x,j}$ to $D^{(c)}$
        \ENDFOR
    \ENDFOR
    \STATE $D^{(c+1)} = D^{(c)}$
\ENDFOR
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Outlier Detection in Embedding Space}

To identify underrepresented data points, we adopt a distance–based outlier detection approach. For each embedding $z_i$, we compute
\[
s(z_i) = \frac{1}{k} \sum_{j=1}^k \lVert z_i - \pi_j(z_i) \rVert_2,
\]
where $\pi_j(z_i)$ is the $j$th nearest neighbor of $z_i$ in the dataset under Euclidean distance. Intuitively, this score grows when a sample lies in a sparse region of the embedding space. We select the $N_{\text{OOD}}$ points with the largest scores as the most underrepresented. This choice ensures that augmentation effort is focused on regions where the model has the least exposure.

\subsection{Diffusion–based Augmentation}

For each OOD sample $x$, we employ Stable Diffusion 2 UnCLIP in frozen form to generate $N_{\text{Aug}}$ new images. The generator conditions on the CLIP embedding of $x$, producing images that preserve high–level semantics while varying in texture, pose, or other attributes. Since the generator is frozen, the process is reproducible and adds no additional trainable parameters to the system.

\subsection{Controlling Dataset Growth}

BRIDGE guarantees predictable dataset growth. Two static hyperparameters fully determine the number of new images created in each cycle:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $N_{\text{OOD}}$: number of OOD datapoints selected per cycle,
    \item $N_{\text{Aug}}$: number of generated images per OOD datapoint.
\end{itemize}
Thus the dataset size evolves as
\[
|D^{(c+1)}| = |D^{(c)}| + N_{\text{OOD}} \times N_{\text{Aug}}.
\]
This property makes the method straightforward to scale and ensures that augmentation does not grow unbounded.

\subsection{Hyperparameters}

Unless otherwise noted, we use $k=100$ for kNN distance computation, $N_{\text{OOD}}=1000$ samples per cycle, $N_{\text{Aug}}=100$ generations per sample, $N_E=20$ epochs per cycle, and $N_C=5$ cycles. These settings create 100,000 new samples per cycle, striking a balance between dataset repair and computational cost.



\section{Experiments}
\label{experiments}

We evaluate BRIDGE on long-tailed variants of ImageNet-100, CIFAR-10, and CIFAR-100, comparing against baselines, analyzing ablations, and benchmarking against state-of-the-art methods. Unless otherwise stated, results are averaged over three seeds.

\subsection{Datasets}

\noindent
\textbf{ImageNet-100-LT.} 
ImageNet-100 is a 100-class subset of ImageNet \citep{tian2020contrastive}. Following prior work \citep{imagenet-lt}, we create ImageNet-100-LT with a long-tailed imbalance analogous to real world datasets by sampling class frequencies from a Pareto distribution with $\alpha=6$, yielding approximately 15k images with a severe imbalance ratio.  

\noindent
\textbf{CIFAR-10-LT and CIFAR-100-LT.} 
We additionally construct long-tailed versions of CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 \citep{krizhevsky2009learning} using the same procedure, enabling controlled comparisons across datasets of different scale and granularity.  

\subsection{Architectures and SSL Methods}

We evaluate three backbones: ResNet-50 (25.6M parameters), ViT-Small (22.1M), and ViT-Base (86.6M). Our main experiments use SimCLR \citepp{chen2020simple}, while ablations include DINO \citepp{DINO} and MoCo \citepp{he2020momentum}.  

\subsection{Downstream Evaluation}

We assess representations with both linear probing and $k$-nearest neighbor (kNN) classification. Downstream datasets include CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, Stanford Cars \citepp{cars}, FGVC Aircraft \citepp{aircraft}, Oxford Flowers \citepp{flowers}, Oxford-IIIT Pets \citepp{pets}, and ImageNet-100-LT itself. This suite covers both coarse and fine-grained recognition.

\subsection{Baselines and State-of-the-Art Comparisons}

We include two baselines: (1) a balanced baseline trained on a uniform subset of ImageNet-100 with the same total size as ImageNet-100-LT, and (2) an imbalanced baseline trained directly on ImageNet-100-LT.  

For state-of-the-art comparisons, we later evaluate against Temperature Schedules (TS; \citep{ts}), SDCLR \citepp{sdclr}, and the combined methods BRIDGE+TS and BRIDGE+SDCLR. Combining is natural because BRIDGE operates at the \emph{data level}, while TS and SDCLR modify the \emph{training dynamics}. BRIDGE augments underrepresented regions of the dataset, whereas TS and SDCLR reshape how contrastive objectives are optimized. Their orthogonality makes integration straightforward: we apply BRIDGE’s augmentation loop and then train with either TS or SDCLR in place of standard SimCLR dynamics.

\subsection{Implementation Details}

We use AdamW with weight decay $1\times10^{-4}$. Unless specified otherwise, the learning rate is $1\times10^{-4}$, temperature $0.1$, no temperature scheduling, $t_{\max}=400$, and warmup of 10 epochs.  

For most experiments (baseline comparisons and ablations), we run \textbf{5 cycles with 20 epochs each}. For state-of-the-art comparisons, we increase the budget to \textbf{8 cycles with 100 epochs each} to match the training regimes used in prior work.  

SSL-specific hyperparameters follow standard practice: SimCLR uses learning rate $1\times10^{-4}$ and temperature $0.07$; DINO uses learning rate $0.03$; MoCo/MoCo-v3 use learning rate $0.03$ and temperature $0.07$; SDCLR uses learning rate $0.5$, temperature $0.07$, and prune rate $0.3$.  

Stable Diffusion 2 UnCLIP is frozen throughout. We select $N_{\text{OOD}}=1000$ samples per cycle with $k=100$ nearest neighbors, and generate $N_{\text{Aug}}=100$ variants for each, producing 100k new images per cycle.


\subsection{Baseline Comparisons}

Table~\ref{table:baseline_comp} compares BRIDGE with balanced and imbalanced baselines using a ViT-B backbone and SimCLR. BRIDGE consistently outperforms both baselines across all datasets. Notably, it improves linear probe accuracy by up to 11.7\% on Oxford Flowers and 7.5\% on Oxford-IIIT Pets compared to the imbalanced baseline, and also improves kNN performance, indicating a more structured feature space.

\subsection{Ablation Studies}

We perform extensive ablations to isolate the contribution of each component:

\noindent\textbf{SSL method.} 
Table~\ref{table:pretraining-abl} shows that SimCLR outperforms DINO and MoCo when combined with BRIDGE, with the largest gains on fine-grained datasets.  

\noindent\textbf{Backbone architecture.} 
Table~\ref{table:backbone_abl} compares ViT-S, ViT-B, and ResNet-50. ResNet-50 achieves the best CIFAR-10 performance (71.2\%), while ViTs perform better on fine-grained datasets, with ViT-B reaching 30.1\% on Oxford-IIIT Pets.  

\noindent\textbf{Sample selection strategy.} 
As shown in Table~\ref{table:sample_selection}, OOD-based selection consistently outperforms uniform sampling, validating our hypothesis that targeting underrepresented regions yields more effective augmentation.  

\noindent\textbf{Sample generation method.} 
Table~\ref{table:sample_generation} compares repopulation (adding back removed samples) with Stable Diffusion generation. Both improve over the baseline, but diffusion yields larger gains, especially on Oxford Flowers. This gives evidence that the specific method for repairing the dataset is not important, instead the idea of detecting underrepresented data and then finding new similar data to augment the original dataset improves representations.

\noindent\textbf{Number of cycles.} 
Table~\ref{table:n_cycle_abl} shows that increasing cycles generally improves performance, with the best CIFAR-10 results at 20 cycles (68.7\%) and the best Oxford-IIIT Pets results at 5 cycles (30.1\%).  

\noindent\textbf{Number of OOD samples.} 
Table~\ref{table:n_samples_abl} analyzes the number of samples selected per cycle. Selecting 500 samples provides the best trade-off, while selecting too many (2500) degrades performance, suggesting that focusing augmentation on the most extreme outliers is most effective.


\section{Conclusion}
Our comprehensive experiments demonstrate that BRIDGE effectively mitigates the negative impact of dataset imbalance in self-supervised learning. By strategically detecting regions of the feature space that are underrepresented and augmenting them with generated samples, our method achieves consistent improvements across different architectures, SSL methods, and downstream tasks. The ablation studies confirm that both OOD-based sample selection and diffusion-based generation contribute significantly to the effectiveness of our approach.
