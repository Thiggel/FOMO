\begin{abstract}
Self-supervised learning inherits the coverage of its source data. When an unlabeled corpus is long-tailed, the learned representation becomes dense around frequent modes and thin where coverage is weak, and a change of training objective adds none of the missing support. \method\ is a label-free feedback loop in which the current encoder scores every source image by mean $k$NN distance, a budgeted acquisition rule selects a sparse but recoverable band of that score distribution, a frozen image-to-image diffusion model adds local variants of the selected anchors, and pretraining continues on the enlarged source set. We formulate acquisition as recoverability-weighted allocation of local support and derive conditions under which a finite budget should favor sparse interior regions and be reallocated after every repair. Across five long-tailed and web-sourced regimes, \method\ gives the best seven-task linear-probe and $k$NN transfer, and paired full-schedule experiments extend the gain to ViT-S under contrastive, self-distillation and masked-reconstruction objectives, to low-label fine-tuning and to semantic segmentation. Matched controls cover uniform and extreme-tail acquisition, conventional augmentation, oracle restoration of withheld real images, language-mediated generation and direct distillation of the generator's features. The acquisition rule has no measurable effect after a single repair stage, where nine score bands lie within 1.32 points, and the same rules separate by 8.0 points over five stages. The benefit is produced by repeated re-estimation of where support is missing.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Self-supervised learning is the standard route to transferable visual representations from unlabeled images, through contrastive learning, self-distillation and masked prediction~\citep{chen2020simple,he2020momentum,DINO,he2022masked,oquab2023dinov2,dinov3}. All of them learn from whatever the source corpus contains. When that corpus is long-tailed, the representation concentrates around frequent modes, covers rare regions weakly and transfers worse~\citep{tian2021divide,assran2210hidden,ts,sdclr}. Prior work has responded on the model side, through a temperature schedule over contrastive training~\citep{ts}, a pruned self-competitor that emphasizes rare samples~\citep{sdclr} or imported external out-of-distribution data~\citep{colt}, and all of it leaves the source set fixed.

We ask whether the source set can be repaired while learning proceeds. After each pretraining stage, \method\ embeds the current source set with the current encoder, scores every image by its mean distance to its $k$ nearest neighbors, selects a sparse but semantically coherent band of that distribution, and generates local variants of the selected images with a frozen image-to-image diffusion model, after which pretraining continues on the enlarged set (Figure~\ref{fig:bridge-overview}). The encoder identifies where its own representation is thin and the generator adds support there. No labels, class frequencies or downstream signals enter the loop.

Two decisions determine whether such a loop helps, where a finite generation budget is spent and whether the allocation is re-estimated after each repair. The largest scores mark a tail populated by artifacts, degenerate crops and images the generator cannot vary faithfully, while uniform sampling spends most of the budget in regions that are already well supported. \method\ restricts acquisition to the 75th to 99th percentile band of the score distribution, centers a candidate pool on the mode of that band and covers the pool with farthest-point sampling. We formalize both decisions as budgeted allocation over regions of representation space with diminishing returns to local support and repairability that falls in the extreme tail. The optimal band is then interior, greedy reallocation after each intervention is optimal, and any rule applied once has an effect bounded by the ratio of budget to existing support.

The last statement describes the experiments. When every score band from the densest quartile to the extreme tail receives the same budget after a single repair stage, the nine seven-task averages lie within 1.32 points, the run-to-run resolution of the pipeline, and the same rules separate by 8.0 points once the loop runs for five stages with re-estimation between them. The contribution of \method\ lies in the feedback, and none of its components produces the effect alone.

\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item We formulate label-free data repair as recoverability-weighted allocation of local support and prove that sparse interior regions carry the largest marginal value, that greedy reallocation after each repair is optimal under diminishing returns, and that any single-stage allocation has an effect bounded by the ratio of budget to existing support.
\item We instantiate this policy in \method, where mean $k$NN distance estimates local support, a trimmed mode window selects a recoverable sparse band, farthest-point sampling limits redundancy and a frozen image-to-image generator supplies variants.
\item We evaluate five source regimes and seven transfer tasks under linear-probe and $k$NN evaluation, with paired ViT-S experiments across four SSL objectives, low-label fine-tuning, semantic segmentation and matched controls for uniform, extreme-tail, one-shot, conventional, oracle, language-mediated and distillation alternatives.
\item We separate the acquisition rule from the loop by running identical rules under one repair stage and under five, and locate the effect in the repeated re-estimation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:background}
\paragraph{Self-supervised learning under skewed sources.}
Contrastive learning, momentum contrast, self-distillation and masked reconstruction have made unlabeled pretraining routine~\citep{chen2020simple,he2020momentum,DINO,oquab2023dinov2,dinov3,he2022masked}, and their sensitivity to source imbalance is established. Divide and Contrast recovers performance on uncurated data by clustering and specializing experts~\citep{tian2021divide}, the hidden uniform cluster prior explains why standard objectives favor balanced sources~\citep{assran2210hidden}, and frequency-aware learning reweights by estimated class frequency~\citep{fassl}. The methods closest to our comparison change the objective on a fixed corpus. SDCLR prunes a self-competitor so that persistently difficult samples receive more gradient~\citep{sdclr}, Temperature Schedules emphasize head and tail at different times~\citep{ts}, and COLT draws external out-of-distribution images into the batch~\citep{colt}. \method\ holds the objective fixed and changes the source set, so it is complementary, and we report the combination with TS and SDCLR.

\paragraph{Density, rarity and representation-driven curation.}
Mean deep nearest-neighbor distance is an effective nonparametric out-of-distribution score in high-dimensional feature spaces~\citep{sun2022out}, and we use it as a local-support estimate on the training set itself. Data-centric work uses representations to decide what to keep. Pruning by prototype distance beats power-law scaling~\citep{sorscher2022beyond}, concept-cluster complexity prunes web-scale corpora~\citep{abbas2024effective}, and data quality is studied in its own right~\citep{whang2023data}. These methods remove examples. SMOTE adds them by interpolating labeled minority examples~\citep{chawla2002smote}, and DOPING oversamples the low-density edge of a learned latent distribution for unsupervised anomaly detection~\citep{lim2018doping}. DOPING is the closest antecedent to acquiring by latent rarity. It targets the extreme edge, uses no image generator and does not recompute its target after the representation changes, and our top-tail control is a DOPING-style rule under our generator and budget.

\paragraph{Budgeted acquisition and coverage.}
Active learning is the formal precedent for spending a finite acquisition budget under a learned representation. The core-set formulation treats acquisition as $k$-center coverage and selects with the greedy farthest-first traversal we use inside the mode window, which carries a factor-two approximation guarantee~\citep{sener2018active,gonzalez1985clustering}, and ProbCover maximizes covered probability mass at a fixed radius~\citep{yehuda2022covering}. \citet{hacohen2022budget} show that the preferred strategy reverses with budget, dense regions being better targets when the budget is small relative to the corpus, which our single-stage sweep reproduces. Active learning acquires labels for existing images, whereas \method\ creates images without labels, and its scoring function is the encoder being trained, so each acquisition changes the function that makes the next one.

\paragraph{Generative augmentation for supervised recognition.}
Denoising diffusion and its latent-space variants~\citep{ho2020denoising,rombach2022highresolution} make image-conditioned editing possible through SDEdit, which adds noise to an image and denoises it under the model's prior~\citep{meng2022sdedit}, and that operator is our repair step. DA-Fusion applies it to few-shot supervised recognition with class prompts and domain adaptation~\citep{trabucco2023effective}. Class-conditional generation at ImageNet scale improves supervised accuracy~\citep{azizi2023synthetic}, and the utility of synthetic data depends on prompt design, filtering, diversity, guidance and the domain gap to the target~\citep{he2023synthetic,fan2024scaling}. TADA, the closest targeted method, identifies examples a supervised classifier learns slowly, generates faithful variants for them, and reports that augmenting a targeted third of the data beats augmenting all of it~\citep{nguyen2026tada}. Every method in this family uses class identity or a supervised loss to decide what to augment, whereas \method\ uses neither, and we adapt the TADA criterion to a label-free learning-speed signal as a direct baseline.

\paragraph{Synthetic data for representation learning.}
Synthetic imagery has served as a training source for self-supervised features since \citet{ren2018crossdomain}. Representations can be learned from procedural noise and image programs~\citep{baradad2021noise,baradad2022procedural}, from the latent neighborhoods of a black-box generator~\citep{jahanian2022generative}, from synthetic ImageNet clones~\citep{sariyildiz2023fake} and from text-to-image samples used as multi-positive views~\citep{tian2023stablerep}. These methods replace or globally supplement the source and allocate no budget over an observed skewed corpus. DiffAug, the closest iterative predecessor, trains a semantic encoder and a conditional diffusion generator jointly so that generated positives improve contrastive learning~\citep{zang2024diffaug}. \method\ holds the generator fixed and asks where it should intervene, and our DiffAug control adapts its objective to our source.

\paragraph{Data engines and external priors.}
Industrial data engines close a loop between a deployed model and its training data. AIDE uses vision-language and language models to diagnose detector failures, curate and auto-label data and verify the result~\citep{liang2024aide}, and platforms such as Omniverse Replicator supply infrastructure for iterating such pipelines~\citep{worker2022omniverse}. Both operate from supervised task failures with annotation in the loop. Our AIDE-style control clusters frozen CLIP features, selects inversely to cluster occupancy, captions the selection and generates from text, and a captioned SDEdit control isolates the value of keeping the image as a condition. A frozen generator also carries knowledge from its own pretraining data. DreamTeacher distills generative features into discriminative backbones~\citep{li2023dreamteacher}, DIFT shows that diffusion activations encode semantic correspondence~\citep{tang2023dift}, and REPA transfers features in the other direction into a diffusion model~\citep{yu2025repa}, so distilling the generator's features directly is a necessary control, which we report. Appendix~\ref{app:prior-art} tabulates the closest methods by supervision, acquisition signal, data action and feedback.

\section{Theoretical Framework}
\label{sec:theory}
We model targeted repair as budgeted allocation over coherent regions of representation space. A region is valuable when additional support reduces its contribution to downstream risk, and a repair is useful only when the generator preserves the semantic content of its anchor, so the policy must weigh support deficit against repairability and redundancy. Let $r_k(z;Z)$ be the distance from $z$ to its $k$th nearest neighbor in $Z$ and let $A$ be a finite set of valid variants generated from $z$. Proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\begin{proposition}[Monotone local support]
\label{prop:local-support}
For any finite $A$, $r_k(z;Z\cup A)\leq r_k(z;Z)$. The inequality is strict when an added point enters the first $k$ neighbors of $z$ and displaces a neighbor at strictly larger distance.
\end{proposition}

To connect support to learning, partition the representation into regions $j$ with current effective support $n_j$ and model the regional contribution to downstream error by
\begin{equation}
\mathcal B(\mathbf m)=\sum_j p_j a_j(n_j+m_j+\kappa)^{-\gamma},
\label{eq:regional-bound}
\end{equation}
where $p_j$ is the transfer-relevant mass of region $j$, $a_j$ its difficulty, $m_j$ the number of valid repairs allocated to it, and $\kappa,\gamma>0$, the usual diminishing-returns form for learning from additional samples. Write $\Delta_j(m_j)=p_ja_j[(n_j+m_j+\kappa)^{-\gamma}-(n_j+m_j+1+\kappa)^{-\gamma}]$ for the reduction from one more valid repair.

\begin{proposition}[Sparse-region marginal value]
\label{prop:sparse-value}
$\Delta_j(m_j)$ is positive and strictly decreasing in $n_j+m_j$. Between two regions of equal transfer mass and difficulty, the region with less effective support has the larger marginal value.
\end{proposition}

A finite budget should therefore be spent away from well-supported regions. Sparsity alone is not sufficient, because generation fails on corrupt or incoherent anchors. Let $d$ be a sparsity score, $b(d)$ the marginal value of a valid repair at that score, $\rho(d)$ the probability of a faithful variant, and $\lambda\geq0$ the cost of an unfaithful one. Expected repair utility is
\begin{equation}
u(d)=\rho(d)\,b(d)-\bigl(1-\rho(d)\bigr)\lambda.
\label{eq:repair-utility}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Interior recoverable-support optimum]
\label{prop:interior}
Suppose $b$ and $\rho$ are positive and differentiable on $[d_{\min},d_{\max}]$ with $b'(d)>0$ and $\rho'(d)<0$, and define $g(d)=b'(d)/(b(d)+\lambda)+\rho'(d)/\rho(d)$. If $g$ is strictly decreasing with $g(d_{\min})>0$ and $g(d_{\max})<0$, then $u$ has a unique maximizer $d^\star$ in the interior, characterized by $g(d^\star)=0$.
\end{proposition}

The condition compares the relative growth of support value against the relative decay of repairability, and its monotonicity assumption on $g$ is close to assuming the conclusion, so Proposition~\ref{prop:closed-form} in the appendix discharges it for $b(d)=b_0d^{\beta}$ with $\beta\in(0,1]$ and $\rho(d)=\rho_0e^{-\gamma d}$, where $g$ is strictly decreasing for every $\lambda\geq0$ and $d^{\star}=\beta/\gamma$ at $\lambda=0$. The optimum moves outward when support value is closer to linear and inward when fidelity decays faster. \method\ operationalizes the interior preference with a trimmed score band and estimates neither parameter. The remaining results concern repetition. Consider a separable objective $U(\mathbf m)=\sum_jU_j(n_j+m_j)$ with each $U_j$ increasing and discretely concave and a budget $\sum_jm_j=B$.

\begin{proposition}[Adaptive allocation under diminishing returns]
\label{prop:adaptive}
For integer allocations, repeatedly assigning one repair to the region with the largest current marginal gain maximizes $U(\mathbf m)$. A fixed allocation becomes suboptimal once a region's marginal gain falls below that of another region.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Single-stage insensitivity]
\label{prop:one-stage}
Let $\mathbf m$ and $\mathbf m'$ be any two allocations of the same budget $B$ under Equation~\ref{eq:regional-bound}, computed from one representation and applied without re-estimation. Then
\begin{equation}
\bigl|\mathcal B(\mathbf m)-\mathcal B(\mathbf m')\bigr|\;\leq\;B\,\gamma\max_j p_ja_j\,(n_j+\kappa)^{-\gamma-1},
\end{equation}
which tends to zero as $B/n_{\min}\to0$ for the least-supported region $n_{\min}$.
\end{proposition}

A policy that re-estimates support after each intervention follows the changing marginal values of Proposition~\ref{prop:adaptive}, while a frozen policy keeps spending according to an ordering the first intervention has invalidated. Proposition~\ref{prop:one-stage} bounds what any rule can achieve when applied once. With 2{,}500 generated images against a source of about 10{,}000, it predicts that no acquisition rule separates from any other after a single stage, and Section~\ref{sec:one-stage} finds every score band within 1.32 points. The separation over five stages lies outside the single-stage model, because re-estimation makes the support entering each score depend on earlier allocations, and we claim no matching lower bound for the repeated regime. Equation~\ref{eq:regional-bound} and the monotone behavior of $b$ and $\rho$ are modeling assumptions, and Section~\ref{sec:results} tests their observable predictions, an interior optimum, a large gap to uniform and extreme-tail acquisition, and a null effect of the rule at one stage.

\section{BRIDGE}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Loop and scoring}
Let $D^{(0)}$ be an unlabeled source set and $f_\theta$ an SSL encoder. \method\ alternates pretraining and repair over $C$ cycles, and at cycle $c$ the encoder is $f_{\theta^{(c)}}$, the source set $D^{(c)}$ and its embedding set $Z^{(c)}$. After each training stage, scoring embeds every current source image and assigns a label-free sparsity score, selection turns that distribution into an anchor set under a generation budget $B$, and repair conditions the frozen generator on the anchors and appends the variants. The encoder is the only learned component, so the loop needs no labels and applies to any objective that yields embeddings. Every cycle continues from the previous encoder with a fresh optimizer schedule and retains all earlier images, so a cycle costs one pass over the grown source set and never a pretraining run from scratch.

After training on $D^{(c)}$ we embed each image, $z_i^{(c)}=f_{\theta^{(c)}}(x_i)$, normalize to unit length, and compute
\begin{equation}
d_i^{(c)}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal N_k(z_i^{(c)};\,Z^{(c)}\setminus\{z_i^{(c)}\})}\lVert z_i^{(c)}-z\rVert_2^2,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal N_k(z;Z)$ is the set of $k$ nearest neighbors of $z$ in $Z$, so a large score marks an image in a sparse part of the current representation. We use $k=100$, and $k$ between 10 and 200 moves the seven-task average by about one point (Section~\ref{sec:ablations}).

\subsection{Sparse and recoverable anchor selection}
\label{sec:mode-window}
\begin{wrapfigure}{r}{0.44\textwidth}
\vspace{-8pt}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.44\textwidth]{figures/mode_window_selection.pdf}
\vspace{-6pt}
\caption{The score distribution of one ImageNet-100-LT run. The mode window centers the candidate pool on the modal bin of the 75th to 99th percentile band (green). The top-tail control spends the same budget in the shaded extreme tail.}
\label{fig:mode-window}
\vspace{-6pt}
\end{wrapfigure}
The extreme tail is populated by artifacts, degenerate crops and images the generator cannot vary faithfully, and uniform sampling spends most of its budget in regions that are already dense. The selector targets a sparse region that is still internally coherent in three steps (Figure~\ref{fig:mode-window}). It restricts attention to $U^{(c)}=\{i\mid q^{(c)}_{0.75}\leq d_i^{(c)}\leq q^{(c)}_{0.99}\}$ and builds a histogram over that band whose modal bin has center $m^{(c)}$. Given a candidate-pool multiplier $\alpha$, it forms the mode-centered pool $C^{(c)}\subseteq U^{(c)}$ of size $\alpha B$ that minimizes $\sum_{i\in C}|d_i^{(c)}-m^{(c)}|$. It then selects the anchor set $S^{(c)}$ of size $B$ by greedy farthest-point sampling in embedding space from that pool. The band excludes the scores where faithful repair is least reliable, the mode favors a region with repeated empirical support over isolated endpoints, and farthest-point sampling keeps the budget off near-duplicates. We use $\alpha=4$ and $B=500$. Section~\ref{sec:ablations} shows that the lower cutoff can move from the 75th to the 1st percentile without measurable change, while uniform and top-tail acquisition cost 6.6 to 8.0 points over five cycles.

\subsection{Local generative repair and continued pretraining}
For each anchor we generate $G=5$ variants with a frozen image-to-image diffusion model $g_\phi$, conditioning on the anchor image under an empty text prompt so that the repair signal comes from the image and never from a class name, and set $D^{(c+1)}=D^{(c)}\cup\{g_\phi(x_i,\epsilon_j)\mid i\in S^{(c)},\,j\in[G]\}$. The noise $\epsilon_j$ indexes independent translations of one anchor, so variants keep coarse structure while changing texture, pose, color or composition, and no generated-image filter is applied. The main configuration is Stable Diffusion 3 Medium with 20 denoising steps, strength 0.6 and output resolution equal to the training crop. With an empty prompt the guidance scale is inert, since at a fixed seed scales 3.0, 5.0 and 7.5 produce bit-identical images and scale 1.0 differs by a mean absolute pixel error of $0.0016$ at 49.7 dB. Five training stages contain four repairs, so a full run adds 10{,}000 images to a source of about 10{,}000. The score estimates support deficit, the mode window enforces recoverability, farthest-point sampling approximates coverage and cyclic re-estimation reallocates the budget after every intervention, the four requirements of Section~\ref{sec:theory}. Algorithm~\ref{alg:bridge} in the appendix gives the loop.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Sources, targets and protocol.}
We pretrain on five unlabeled source regimes, the long-tailed ImageNet-100-LT~\citep{imagenet-lt}, CIFAR-10-LT and CIFAR-100-LT~\citep{krizhevsky2009learning}, and 10k subsets of the web corpora PASS~\citep{pass} and DiffusionDB~\citep{diffusiondb}. Transfer is measured on seven benchmarks, CIFAR-10-LT, CIFAR-100-LT, Stanford Cars~\citep{cars}, FGVC Aircraft~\citep{aircraft}, Oxford Flowers~\citep{flowers}, Oxford-IIIT Pets~\citep{pets} and ImageNet-100-LT, by linear probe and by $k$NN classification on frozen features. Every number is a three-seed mean and standard deviation, and paired comparisons share seeds and source checkpoints. ResNet-50 is the default backbone because it is common to the baselines, and \method\ trains five cycles of 100 epochs with $k=100$, $B=500$ and $G=5$. DINO and MAE follow their published ViT recipes and never run on a convolutional backbone. Because the downstream tasks are heterogeneous, we report per-task tables and use the seven-task average as a summary, and Appendix~\ref{app:implementation} gives implementation details.

\paragraph{Compared methods.}
The source-regime comparison covers source-matched SimCLR, TS, SDCLR and \method, with hybrids in the appendix. All ablations use ImageNet-100-LT source pretraining, the same downstream suite and three seeds, and vary one component at a time. The generation ablation includes oracle re-population, which returns the exact real images withheld when the long tail was constructed and so places true support where the method can only estimate that it is missing. That arm is the ceiling available to any repair operator at this budget and is read as an upper bound throughout. The one-stage controls of Section~\ref{sec:one-stage} and Appendix~\ref{app:prior-art} branch from one source checkpoint, add the same number of images and receive the same optimizer updates.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
\subsection{Transfer across source regimes}
Table~\ref{tab:main_sota_label_sources} reports the three label-derived long-tailed sources, and \method\ has the best seven-task average in all three. Under CIFAR-100-LT it raises the average from 23.8 for source-matched SimCLR to 34.4 and is best on every downstream dataset, under ImageNet-100-LT from 39.8 to 42.5 with TS competitive only on the in-domain target, and under CIFAR-10-LT from 29.1 to 34.2. Under $k$NN evaluation the ranking holds, 31.8 to 33.6, 24.0 to 28.7 and 20.9 to 27.0 (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:main_sota_label_sources_knn}), so the gain is present in the neighborhood structure of the features without a learned probe.

The two web sources behave the same way (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:main_sota_web_sources}). On PASS-10k, a natural web corpus, \method\ improves the linear-probe average over SimCLR from 36.8 to 39.9 and the $k$NN average from 29.3 to 30.6, and on DiffusionDB-10k, whose images are themselves synthetic, from 37.7 to 40.1 and from 28.2 to 29.9. \subsection{Beyond the default protocol}
Figure~\ref{fig:objective-deltas} tests whether the effect is specific to SimCLR on ResNet-50 or to frozen-feature evaluation. Four objectives run paired on ViT-S under one schedule and one repair budget, covering contrastive, momentum-contrast, self-distillation and masked-reconstruction pretraining, with both arms of each pair branching from one source checkpoint and receiving the same post-branch updates. Counting linear-probe and $k$NN accuracy on seven datasets gives 14 measurements per objective. \method\ improves 12 of 14 for SimCLR, 12 for MoCo v3, 13 for DINO and 12 for MAE, with mean changes of $+1.32$, $+1.31$, $+1.88$ and $+1.38$ points and a two-sided sign test at $p\leq.013$ in every case. \subsection{Ablations and controls}
\label{sec:ablations}
\input{tables/main_mechanism}
\paragraph{Selection rule.}
Table~\ref{tab:main_mechanism} compares three mode windows with different lower cutoffs against top-tail and uniform acquisition over five cycles, all with the same generator, budget and updates. The three windows reach 42.1 to 42.3 and do not differ detectably, while uniform reaches $35.5\pm1.2$ and top-tail $34.3\pm1.2$, and every window is ahead of both after Holm correction over the eight comparisons at adjusted $p\leq.011$. The exact lower percentile is therefore not the operative choice, and we do not claim that 75 to 99 is optimal. Trimming the extreme tail and diversifying within the remaining sparse band carries the 6.6 to 8.0 points.


\paragraph{Is the gain transfer from the generator?}
A frozen generator trained on external data carries information the source corpus lacks, so an improvement could reflect distillation from it and nothing about where the budget was spent. The five arms of the selection block call the identical Stable Diffusion 3 checkpoint at identical settings, add the same number of images and receive the same updates, and only the anchors differ. An account in which the generator's prior supplies the benefit predicts no spread across those arms, and the observed spread is 8.0 points. A direct control in Appendix Table~\ref{tab:closest-prior-empirical} distills the generator's features into the encoder without any generated image and does not reproduce the effect of targeted repair, while adding \method\ to that distillation recovers it.

\paragraph{Generation mechanism.}
Oracle re-population reaches 40.7 on the seven-task average against 41.5 for generated repair and 35.0 for FLUX at its original sampling settings (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:app_generation_linear}). Generated repair lands within a point of an operator that inserts the true missing images, so estimating where support is missing costs little relative to knowing it, and the FLUX gap is a sampling-budget artifact. Under matched settings FLUX.1-schnell at guidance 3 reaches $19.68\pm0.81$ on the four-task diagnostic against $19.70\pm0.35$ for SD3 at 20 steps (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:generator-sensitivity}), so no generator is intrinsically stronger in our setting. Strength between 0.3 and 0.9 moves that diagnostic by one point and step count from 6 to 20 by 1.1.

\paragraph{Sensitivity, cycles and backbone.}
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:selector-robustness-full-linear} varies the candidate-pool multiplier, upper cutoff, neighborhood size, distance metric and selection strategy one at a time under a single repair stage, and over $k\in\{10,25,50,100,200\}$ the seven-task average moves between 26.80 and 27.84. The table carries its own resolution scale, since cosine scoring rescales the normalized $L_2$ score by a positive constant and selects the identical anchor set, and those two rows differ by up to 1.3 points. At fixed total repair volume and optimizer budget, five cycles improve the seven-task averages over two by 7.3 points under linear probing and 5.7 under $k$NN and remain stronger than ten, so more frequent repair with less learning between repairs does not help monotonically. Across backbones at the default protocol, ResNet-50 leads at 42.3 against 34.8 for ViT-B, while the paired ViT-S experiments above show that the repair effect is present on transformers.

\paragraph{Cost of the loop.}
Against the no-repair run at an identical update budget, the loop raises wall-clock time by 29 to 46 percent across the three source regimes, almost all of it diffusion sampling (Appendix~\ref{app:compute}). The cost scales with the anchor budget and the variants per anchor, is independent of the cycle count at fixed total budget, and stays fixed while the pretraining cost grows with the source. It is unoptimized. The sampler runs at its default step count, variants are never cached, and a distilled few-step generator would reduce the dominant term directly.

Appendix Table~\ref{tab:closest-prior-empirical} places \method\ beside a label-free adaptation of TADA, an AIDE-style vision-language pipeline, inverse-cluster-frequency acquisition, a DiffAug adaptation and direct feature distillation under the same single-stage protocol, and the six comparable pipelines span 1.67 points. \FloatBarrier
\section{Limitations}
\method\ depends on informative encoder neighborhoods in the first cycle and on faithful local generation, and its frozen generator imports the coverage and biases of its own pretraining data. The score band, neighborhood size and budget are operating choices whose defaults transferred across five sources without tuning, and no optimum has been shown for any of them. The fixed-encoder test verifies where support is inserted without establishing how every learned neighborhood evolves, and the learned-space local effect is mixed for SimCLR. Later cycles may select generated images as anchors, no generated-image filter is applied, and a benefit for every objective and target remains unshown. Effectiveness beyond 10k-image web subsets, and whether the fixed budget must grow with the source, remain open.

\section{Conclusion}
Skewed-data SSL can be treated as a feedback problem in which the learner allocates a finite budget to change its own future source distribution. Under diminishing returns to local support and fidelity that decays in the extreme tail, the budget belongs in a sparse interior band and should be reallocated after each repair. \method\ implements that policy without labels and improves transfer over source-matched baselines and over uniform, extreme-tail, one-shot and language-mediated alternatives on five sources, four objectives and two backbones. Where the budget is spent has no measurable effect for one intervention and an effect of 8.0 points once the representation that chooses the target has itself been repaired.

\section{Proofs for the Support-Allocation Framework}
\label{app:proofs}

\begin{proposition}[Closed-form interior optimum]
\label{prop:closed-form}
Let $b(d)=b_0d^{\beta}$ with $b_0>0$ and $\beta\in(0,1]$, and let $\rho(d)=\rho_0e^{-\gamma d}$ with $\gamma>0$. Then $g$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,\infty)$ for every $\lambda\geq0$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:interior} applies whenever $g$ changes sign on the interval. If $\lambda=0$ the maximizer is $d^{\star}=\beta/\gamma$, independent of $b_0$ and $\rho_0$.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:local-support}.}
Sort the distances from $z$ to points in $Z$ and denote their $k$th order statistic by $r_k(z;Z)$. Adding $A$ preserves every old candidate distance and may introduce smaller ones. The $k$th order statistic can therefore only decrease. It decreases strictly when an added distance enters the first $k$ positions and displaces a strictly larger distance. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse-value}.}
Let $x=n_j+m_j+\kappa$. Since $f(x)=x^{-\gamma}$ is positive, strictly decreasing and strictly convex for $x>0$, the forward difference $f(x)-f(x+1)$ is positive and strictly decreases with $x$. Multiplication by $p_ja_j>0$ preserves both properties. Equal $p_ja_j$ therefore leaves effective support as the sole determinant of the marginal ordering. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:interior}.}
Rewrite the utility as $u(d)=\rho(d)(b(d)+\lambda)-\lambda$. Its derivative is
\[
u'(d)=\rho(d)(b(d)+\lambda)\left[\frac{b'(d)}{b(d)+\lambda}+\frac{\rho'(d)}{\rho(d)}\right]=\rho(d)(b(d)+\lambda)\,g(d).
\]
The factor preceding $g(d)$ is positive. Since $g$ is strictly decreasing and changes sign across the interval, it has exactly one root $d^\star$ by continuity. Hence $u$ increases before $d^\star$ and decreases after it, making $d^\star$ the unique global maximizer. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:closed-form}.}
With $b(d)=b_0d^{\beta}$ and $\rho(d)=\rho_0e^{-\gamma d}$ we have $\rho'(d)/\rho(d)=-\gamma$, so
\[
g(d)=\frac{b_0\beta d^{\beta-1}}{b_0d^{\beta}+\lambda}-\gamma=\frac{\beta d^{\beta-1}}{d^{\beta}+c}-\gamma,\qquad c=\lambda/b_0\geq0.
\]
Write $h(d)=\beta d^{\beta-1}(d^{\beta}+c)^{-1}$. Differentiating,
\[
h'(d)=\frac{\beta d^{\beta-2}\bigl[(\beta-1)(d^{\beta}+c)-\beta d^{\beta}\bigr]}{(d^{\beta}+c)^{2}}=\frac{\beta d^{\beta-2}\bigl[(\beta-1)c-d^{\beta}\bigr]}{(d^{\beta}+c)^{2}}.
\]
For $\beta\in(0,1]$ we have $(\beta-1)c\leq0<d^{\beta}$ for all $d>0$, so the bracket is strictly negative and $h'(d)<0$. Hence $g=h-\gamma$ is strictly decreasing for every $\lambda\geq0$. When $\lambda=0$ we have $c=0$ and $g(d)=\beta/d-\gamma$, whose unique root is $d^{\star}=\beta/\gamma$. Since $g$ is positive below it and negative above it, $d^{\star}$ is the maximizer by the argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:interior}. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:adaptive}.}
For region $j$ define the marginal sequence $\delta_{j,t}=U_j(n_j+t)-U_j(n_j+t-1)$ for $t\geq1$. Discrete concavity makes each sequence nonincreasing. Any feasible allocation of $B$ repairs selects a prefix of length $m_j$ from each sequence. Greedy allocation repeatedly chooses the largest available next element, and after $B$ steps it has selected the $B$ largest prefix-feasible marginals. If another allocation had larger utility, it would contain a selected marginal smaller than an available unselected marginal, and exchanging them would improve that allocation, which contradicts its optimality. A frozen allocation can fail this rule because it preserves the initial ordering after selected regions have moved to smaller marginals. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:one-stage}.}
Write $f(x)=x^{-\gamma}$. For $x>0$ the mean value theorem gives $f(x)-f(x+1)=\gamma\xi^{-\gamma-1}$ for some $\xi\in(x,x+1)$, so
\[
0<\Delta_j(m_j)=p_ja_j\bigl[f(n_j+m_j+\kappa)-f(n_j+m_j+1+\kappa)\bigr]\leq p_ja_j\gamma(n_j+\kappa)^{-\gamma-1},
\]
using $\xi>n_j+m_j+\kappa\geq n_j+\kappa$ and $m_j\geq0$. Both $\mathcal B(\mathbf m)$ and $\mathcal B(\mathbf m')$ are obtained from the common baseline $\mathcal B(\mathbf 0)$ by subtracting sums of at most $B$ such marginals, since $\sum_jm_j=\sum_jm_j'=B$. Each sum lies in $[0,\,B\gamma\max_jp_ja_j(n_j+\kappa)^{-\gamma-1}]$, and the difference of two numbers drawn from an interval is bounded by its length. Monotonicity of $(n+\kappa)^{-\gamma-1}$ in $n$ gives the $n_{\min}$ form. \qed

\section{Implementation and Reproducibility}
\label{app:implementation}

\paragraph{Training schedule.} The encoder is warm-started from the previous stage at every cycle and is never retrained from scratch. Each stage uses a fresh optimizer and learning-rate schedule. All earlier real and generated images remain in the source set, so the setting differs from sequential-task learning in which previous data disappear. Five training stages contain four repairs, since generation runs only when another stage follows. The cycle ablation holds the total optimizer update budget and the total number of generated images fixed and compares fewer repairs with more learning between them against more frequent repairs with less.

\paragraph{Core configuration.}
The main experiments use ResNet-50 encoders, five cycles of 100 epochs, mean $k$NN distance with $k=100$, 500 anchors per cycle and five variants per anchor. The optimizer is AdamW with weight decay $10^{-4}$, and method-specific learning rates and temperatures follow the settings of each baseline. The DINO arm uses the reference recipe with a ViT-S backbone, two global and eight local crops at $96\times96$ and a bottleneck projection head. MAE is ViT-only and runs five cycles of 80 epochs.

\paragraph{Scoring and selection.}
The submitted experiments used raw squared $\ell_2$ distance for scoring and normalized features for farthest-point sampling. Because raw feature norms can confound cross-objective comparisons, the controlled experiments use normalized $\ell_2$ throughout and report raw $\ell_2$, normalized $\ell_2$, cosine and multiscale-$k$ variants as a robustness study. The mode-window selector restricts the score distribution to the 75th to 99th percentile range, builds a histogram with the automatic bin heuristic, forms a mode-centered candidate pool of size $4B$ and applies greedy farthest-point sampling to select the final $B$ anchors.

\paragraph{Generators.}
The main method uses Stable Diffusion 3 Medium in image-to-image mode. The generation ablation replaces it with FLUX or with oracle re-population, which adds the withheld real images and runs no generator. In the PASS and DiffusionDB experiments the SD3 batch size is 12 to fit the 24-hour wall-time limit.

\paragraph{A corrected DINO recipe.} The DINO arm was rebuilt after we found that an earlier implementation omitted the bottleneck projection head and applied solarization to the wrong crops. Its baseline is competitive with SimCLR and MoCo v3 on the same schedule.

\paragraph{A checkpointing fault and the reruns it forced.} Each cycle constructs a fresh trainer whose step counter restarts at zero and stops at the same step limit. The checkpoint callback, reused across cycles, treated a save at a step it had already written as a duplicate and skipped it, so runs configured with a per-cycle step cap published their first-cycle encoder however far later cycles diverged. We found this when two policy conditions reproduced each other to the last bit while their anchor manifests overlapped on 14 of 500 anchors from the second cycle onward. Every paired ViT-S cell behind Figure~\ref{fig:objective-deltas} was rerun with the callback state reset between cycles, 19 runs and 416 wall-clock hours, and we verify before publication that no two conditions in a table share an encoder fingerprint. Runs whose cycles end on an epoch boundary were never affected.

\paragraph{Source data.}
PASS and DiffusionDB use \texttt{train[:10000]} with pretraining split override \texttt{[1.0, 0.0, 0.0]}. The long-tailed ImageNet-100, CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 sources use their standard long-tailed configurations without the 10k restriction.

\paragraph{Seeds and reporting.}
All claims rest on three-seed averages reported as mean and standard deviation. Paired comparisons share seeds and, where stated, source checkpoints. Multiple comparisons within one table are corrected by the Holm procedure.

\paragraph{Code availability.}
An anonymous code release is available at \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/status/FOMO-0488}.

\section{Compute Resources and Runtime}
\label{app:compute}
Most experiments use one node with two NVIDIA A100 80GB GPUs under a 24-hour wall-time limit. The DINO arm fits on a single 48GB card at the batch size used. The 454 completed runs retained for this paper total approximately 5{,}471 wall-clock hours. Runs on two-GPU nodes consume roughly twice that in GPU hours, while the paired ViT-S reruns hold a single card each. The 19 reruns that replaced encoders affected by the checkpointing fault account for 416 hours, and the baselines and controls that appear only in the appendix for a further 2{,}987. These totals exclude failed runs, timeouts, cache migration and pilot jobs, so the project consumed more compute than the successful totals alone.

Representative mean wall-clock times over three seeds are as follows. On ImageNet-100-LT, SimCLR takes 16.9 hours and \method\ 21.8. On PASS, 10.2 and 14.9. On DiffusionDB, 11.6 and 16.4. The loop therefore raises wall-clock time by 29 to 46 percent over the corresponding no-repair run, of which the embedding pass is a small fraction and diffusion sampling the remainder. Four repairs add 10{,}000 images in total, which is 100 percent of a 10k source, 10 percent of a 100k source and 1 percent of a one-million-image source. This describes the relative cost under a fixed budget and does not establish that the fixed budget remains effective at those scales.

\section{Broader Impact}
\label{app:impact}
A method that improves representation quality under biased source data can reduce the need for manual relabeling and can make SSL more useful where balanced data are difficult to collect. The method also relies on large image generators and web-sourced datasets, which carry risks related to license compliance, privacy and unintended memorization or harmful content. Our experiments stay within existing dataset and model release terms and release no new generators or scraped collections. Any deployment would require a task-specific review of fairness, privacy and content safety.

\section{Algorithm}
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\method\ training loop with mode-window selection}
\label{alg:bridge}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Initialize source set $D^{(0)}$, encoder $f_{\theta^{(0)}}$, frozen generator $g_{\phi}$, stage lengths $\{E_c\}_{c=0}^{C-1}$
\FOR{$c = 0, \dots, C-1$}
    \STATE Continue training $f_{\theta^{(c)}}$ on $D^{(c)}$ for $E_c$ epochs with a fresh optimizer schedule
    \IF{$c<C-1$}
        \STATE Compute normalized embeddings $Z^{(c)} = \{f_{\theta^{(c)}}(x_i) \mid x_i \in D^{(c)}\}$
        \STATE Compute mean $k$NN scores $\{d_i^{(c)}\}$ from $Z^{(c)}$
        \STATE Restrict to the 75th to 99th percentile band and locate the modal bin center $m^{(c)}$
        \STATE Form the candidate pool $C^{(c)}$ of size $\alpha B$ whose scores are closest to $m^{(c)}$
        \STATE Select $S^{(c)} \subseteq C^{(c)}$ with $|S^{(c)}| = B$ by greedy farthest-point sampling in embedding space
        \STATE Form $\widetilde{D}^{(c)} = \{g_{\phi}(x_i, \epsilon_j) \mid i \in S^{(c)},\, j \in [G]\}$ and set $D^{(c+1)} = D^{(c)} \cup \widetilde{D}^{(c)}$
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE Return the final encoder $f_{\theta^{(C-1)}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
