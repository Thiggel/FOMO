\begin{abstract}
Self-supervised learning inherits the coverage of its source data. When an unlabeled corpus is long-tailed, the learned representation becomes dense around frequent modes and thin where coverage is weak, and a change of training objective adds none of the missing support. We introduce \method, Balancing Representations by Identifying Deficits and Generating Examples, a label-free feedback loop in which the current encoder scores every source image by its mean $k$NN distance, a budgeted acquisition rule selects a sparse but recoverable band of that score distribution, a frozen image-to-image diffusion model adds local variants of the selected anchors, and pretraining continues on the enlarged source set. We formulate acquisition as recoverability-weighted allocation of local support and derive the conditions under which a finite budget should favor sparse interior regions and be reallocated after every repair. Across five long-tailed and web-sourced regimes, \method\ gives the best seven-task linear-probe and $k$NN transfer, and paired experiments extend the gain to ViT-S under contrastive, self-distillation and masked-reconstruction objectives, to low-label fine-tuning and to semantic segmentation. Generated repair matches an oracle that restores the real images withheld when the long tail was built, and under a frozen encoder the inserted images shrink the neighborhoods of the selected anchors by twice the amount observed at controls matched on initial sparsity.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Self-supervised learning is the standard route to transferable visual representations from unlabeled images~\citep{chen2020simple,he2020momentum,DINO,he2022masked,oquab2023dinov2,dinov3}, and every objective learns from whatever the source corpus contains. When that corpus is long-tailed, the representation concentrates around frequent modes, covers rare regions weakly and transfers worse~\citep{tian2021divide,assran2210hidden,ts,sdclr}. Prior work has responded on the model side, with a temperature schedule~\citep{ts}, a pruned self-competitor~\citep{sdclr} or imported external data~\citep{colt}, and all of it leaves the source set fixed.

We ask whether the source set can be repaired while learning proceeds. \method, Balancing Representations by Identifying Deficits and Generating Examples, embeds the current source set with the current encoder after each pretraining stage, scores every image by its mean distance to its $k$ nearest neighbors, selects a sparse but semantically coherent band of that distribution, and generates local variants of the selected images with a frozen image-to-image diffusion model, after which pretraining continues on the enlarged set (Figure~\ref{fig:bridge-overview}). The encoder identifies where its own representation is thin and the generator adds support there. No labels, class frequencies or downstream signals enter the loop, and Section~\ref{sec:geometry} verifies the mechanism directly, since under a frozen encoder the generated images shrink the selected anchors' neighborhoods by twice the amount observed at matched untreated images.

Two decisions determine whether such a loop helps, where a finite generation budget is spent and whether the allocation is re-estimated after each repair. \method\ restricts acquisition to the 75th to 99th percentile band of the score distribution, centers a candidate pool on the mode of that band and covers the pool with farthest-point sampling. We formalize both decisions as budgeted allocation over regions of representation space with diminishing returns to local support and repairability that falls in the extreme tail. The optimal band is then interior, greedy reallocation after each intervention is optimal, and any rule applied once has an effect bounded by the ratio of budget to existing support. The experiments confirm the last statement, since nine score bands from the densest quartile to the extreme tail are indistinguishable after one repair stage and the same rules separate by 8.0 points over five.

\begin{itemize}
\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
\item We formulate label-free data repair as recoverability-weighted allocation of local support and prove that sparse interior regions carry the largest marginal value, that greedy reallocation after each repair is optimal under diminishing returns, and that any single-stage allocation has an effect bounded by the ratio of budget to existing support.
\item We instantiate this policy in \method, where mean $k$NN distance estimates local support, a trimmed mode window selects a recoverable sparse band, farthest-point sampling limits redundancy and a frozen image-to-image generator supplies variants.
\item We evaluate five source regimes and seven transfer tasks under linear-probe and $k$NN evaluation, with paired ViT-S experiments across four SSL objectives, low-label fine-tuning, semantic segmentation, an oracle that restores withheld real images, and matched controls for the closest acquisition rules, repair operators and generator priors.
\item We separate the acquisition rule from the loop by running identical rules under one repair stage and under five, verify where the generated support lands under a frozen encoder, and locate the effect in the repeated re-estimation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:background}
\paragraph{Self-supervised learning under skewed sources.}
The sensitivity of contrastive, self-distillation and masked-reconstruction pretraining~\citep{chen2020simple,he2020momentum,DINO,oquab2023dinov2,dinov3,he2022masked} to source imbalance is established. Divide and Contrast clusters uncurated data into specialized experts~\citep{tian2021divide}, the hidden uniform cluster prior explains why standard objectives favor balanced sources~\citep{assran2210hidden}, and FASSL reweights by estimated class frequency~\citep{fassl}. The methods closest to our comparison change the objective on a fixed corpus, SDCLR through a pruned self-competitor that gives persistently difficult samples more gradient~\citep{sdclr}, Temperature Schedules by emphasizing head and tail at different times~\citep{ts}, and COLT by drawing external out-of-distribution images into the batch~\citep{colt}. \method\ holds the objective fixed and changes the source set, so it is complementary, and we report the combination with TS and SDCLR.

\paragraph{Density, rarity and representation-driven curation.}
Mean deep nearest-neighbor distance is an effective nonparametric out-of-distribution score in high-dimensional feature spaces~\citep{sun2022out}, and we use it as a local-support estimate on the training set itself. Representation-driven curation prunes by prototype distance~\citep{sorscher2022beyond} or concept-cluster complexity~\citep{abbas2024effective} and treats data quality as a subject in its own right~\citep{whang2023data}, and all of it removes examples. SMOTE adds them by interpolating labeled minority examples~\citep{chawla2002smote}, and DOPING oversamples the low-density edge of a learned latent distribution for unsupervised anomaly detection~\citep{lim2018doping}. DOPING is the closest antecedent to acquiring by latent rarity, targets the extreme edge, uses no image generator and never recomputes its target, and our top-tail control is a DOPING-style rule under our generator and budget.

\paragraph{Budgeted acquisition and coverage.}
Active learning is the formal precedent for spending a finite acquisition budget under a learned representation, and the core-set formulation treats acquisition as $k$-center coverage and selects with the greedy farthest-first traversal we use inside the mode window, which carries a factor-two approximation guarantee~\citep{sener2018active,gonzalez1985clustering}, while ProbCover maximizes covered probability mass at a fixed radius~\citep{yehuda2022covering}. \citet{hacohen2022budget} show that the preferred strategy reverses with budget, dense regions being better targets when the budget is small relative to the corpus, which our single-stage sweep reproduces. Active learning acquires labels for existing images, whereas \method\ creates images without labels and its scoring function is the encoder being trained.

\paragraph{Generative augmentation for supervised recognition.}
SDEdit adds noise to an image and denoises it under a diffusion prior~\citep{ho2020denoising,rombach2022highresolution,meng2022sdedit}, and that operator is our repair step. DA-Fusion applies it to few-shot supervised recognition with class prompts and domain adaptation~\citep{trabucco2023effective}. Class-conditional generation improves supervised ImageNet accuracy~\citep{azizi2023synthetic}, with a utility that depends on prompting, filtering, diversity and domain gap~\citep{he2023synthetic,fan2024scaling}. TADA, the closest targeted method, identifies examples a supervised classifier learns slowly, generates faithful variants for them, and reports that augmenting a targeted third of the data beats augmenting all of it~\citep{nguyen2026tada}. Every method in this family uses class identity or a supervised loss to decide what to augment and decides once. \method\ uses neither and decides again after every repair, and we adapt the TADA criterion to a label-free learning-speed signal as a direct baseline.

\paragraph{Synthetic data for representation learning.}
Synthetic imagery has served as a training source for self-supervised features since \citet{ren2018crossdomain}, from procedural noise and image programs~\citep{baradad2021noise,baradad2022procedural}, latent neighborhoods of a black-box generator~\citep{jahanian2022generative}, synthetic ImageNet clones~\citep{sariyildiz2023fake} and text-to-image samples used as multi-positive views~\citep{tian2023stablerep}. These methods replace or globally supplement the source and allocate no budget over an observed skewed corpus. DiffAug, the closest iterative predecessor, trains a semantic encoder and a conditional diffusion generator jointly so that generated positives improve contrastive learning~\citep{zang2024diffaug}. Its generator must be learned from the same small skewed source it is meant to repair, whereas \method\ holds a pretrained generator fixed and asks where it should intervene, and our DiffAug control adapts its objective to our source.

\paragraph{Data engines and external priors.}
Industrial data engines close a loop between a deployed model and its training data. AIDE uses vision-language and language models to diagnose detector failures, curate and auto-label data and verify the result~\citep{liang2024aide}, and Omniverse Replicator supplies infrastructure for iterating such pipelines~\citep{worker2022omniverse}. Both operate from supervised task failures with annotation in the loop and acquire by the occupancy of semantic clusters, which our inverse cluster-frequency control reproduces under the same generator. A frozen generator also carries knowledge from its own pretraining data, which DreamTeacher distills into discriminative backbones~\citep{li2023dreamteacher}, DIFT reads out as semantic correspondence~\citep{tang2023dift} and REPA transfers in the other direction~\citep{yu2025repa}, so distilling the generator's features directly is a necessary control, which we report. Appendix~\ref{app:prior-art} tabulates the closest methods by supervision, acquisition signal, data action and feedback.

\section{Theoretical Framework}
\label{sec:theory}
We model targeted repair as budgeted allocation over coherent regions of representation space. A repair is useful only when it adds support where downstream risk is concentrated and the generator preserves the semantic content of its anchor, so the policy must weigh support deficit against repairability and redundancy. Let $r_k(z;Z)$ be the distance from $z$ to its $k$th nearest neighbor in $Z$ and let $A$ be a finite set of valid variants generated from $z$. Proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\begin{proposition}[Monotone local support]
\label{prop:local-support}
For any finite $A$, $r_k(z;Z\cup A)\leq r_k(z;Z)$. The inequality is strict when an added point enters the first $k$ neighbors of $z$ and displaces a neighbor at strictly larger distance.
\end{proposition}

To connect support to learning, partition the representation into regions $j$ with current effective support $n_j$ and model the regional contribution to downstream error by
\begin{equation}
\mathcal B(\mathbf m)=\sum_j p_j a_j(n_j+m_j+\kappa)^{-\gamma},
\label{eq:regional-bound}
\end{equation}
where $p_j$ is the transfer-relevant mass of region $j$, $a_j$ its difficulty, $m_j$ the number of valid repairs allocated to it, and $\kappa,\gamma>0$, the usual diminishing-returns form for learning from additional samples. Write $\Delta_j(m_j)=p_ja_j[(n_j+m_j+\kappa)^{-\gamma}-(n_j+m_j+1+\kappa)^{-\gamma}]$ for the reduction from one more valid repair.

\begin{proposition}[Sparse-region marginal value]
\label{prop:sparse-value}
$\Delta_j(m_j)$ is positive and strictly decreasing in $n_j+m_j$. Between two regions of equal transfer mass and difficulty, the region with less effective support has the larger marginal value.
\end{proposition}

Sparsity alone is not a sufficient target, because generation fails on corrupt or incoherent anchors. Let $d$ be a sparsity score, $b(d)$ the marginal value of a valid repair at that score, $\rho(d)$ the probability of a faithful variant, and $\lambda\geq0$ the cost of an unfaithful one. Expected repair utility is
\begin{equation}
u(d)=\rho(d)\,b(d)-\bigl(1-\rho(d)\bigr)\lambda.
\label{eq:repair-utility}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Interior recoverable-support optimum]
\label{prop:interior}
Suppose $b$ and $\rho$ are positive and differentiable on $[d_{\min},d_{\max}]$ with $b'(d)>0$ and $\rho'(d)<0$, and define $g(d)=b'(d)/(b(d)+\lambda)+\rho'(d)/\rho(d)$. If $g$ is strictly decreasing with $g(d_{\min})>0$ and $g(d_{\max})<0$, then $u$ has a unique maximizer $d^\star$ in the interior, characterized by $g(d^\star)=0$.
\end{proposition}

The condition compares the relative growth of support value against the relative decay of repairability, and since its monotonicity assumption on $g$ is close to assuming the conclusion, Proposition~\ref{prop:closed-form} in the appendix discharges it for $b(d)=b_0d^{\beta}$ with $\beta\in(0,1]$ and $\rho(d)=\rho_0e^{-\gamma d}$, where $g$ is strictly decreasing for every $\lambda\geq0$ and $d^{\star}=\beta/\gamma$ at $\lambda=0$. The optimum moves outward when support value is closer to linear and inward when fidelity decays faster. \method\ operationalizes the interior preference with a trimmed score band and estimates neither parameter. The remaining results concern repetition, so consider a separable objective $U(\mathbf m)=\sum_jU_j(n_j+m_j)$ with each $U_j$ increasing and discretely concave and a budget $\sum_jm_j=B$.

\begin{proposition}[Adaptive allocation under diminishing returns]
\label{prop:adaptive}
For integer allocations, repeatedly assigning one repair to the region with the largest current marginal gain maximizes $U(\mathbf m)$. A fixed allocation becomes suboptimal once a region's marginal gain falls below that of another region.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Single-stage insensitivity]
\label{prop:one-stage}
Let $\mathbf m$ and $\mathbf m'$ be any two allocations of the same budget $B$ under Equation~\ref{eq:regional-bound}, computed from one representation and applied without re-estimation. Then
\begin{equation}
\bigl|\mathcal B(\mathbf m)-\mathcal B(\mathbf m')\bigr|\;\leq\;B\,\gamma\max_j p_ja_j\,(n_j+\kappa)^{-\gamma-1},
\end{equation}
which tends to zero as $B/n_{\min}\to0$ for the least-supported region $n_{\min}$.
\end{proposition}

With 2{,}500 generated images against a source of about 10{,}000, Proposition~\ref{prop:one-stage} predicts that no acquisition rule separates from any other after a single stage, which Section~\ref{sec:one-stage} confirms. The five-stage separation lies outside this single-stage model, since re-estimation makes each score depend on earlier allocations, and we claim no matching lower bound for the repeated regime. Equation~\ref{eq:regional-bound} and the monotone behavior of $b$ and $\rho$ are modeling assumptions, and Section~\ref{sec:results} tests their observable predictions, an interior optimum, a large gap to uniform and extreme-tail acquisition, a null effect of the rule at one stage, and added support at the selected anchors.

\section{BRIDGE: Balancing Representations by Identifying Deficits and Generating Examples}
\label{sec:method}
\subsection{Loop and scoring}
Let $D^{(0)}$ be an unlabeled source set and $f_\theta$ an SSL encoder. \method\ alternates pretraining and repair over $C$ cycles, and at cycle $c$ the encoder is $f_{\theta^{(c)}}$, the source set $D^{(c)}$ and its embedding set $Z^{(c)}$. After each training stage, scoring embeds every current source image and assigns a label-free sparsity score, selection turns that distribution into an anchor set under a generation budget $B$, and repair conditions the frozen generator on the anchors and appends the variants. The encoder is the only learned component, so the loop applies to any objective that yields embeddings. Every cycle continues from the previous encoder with a fresh optimizer schedule and retains all earlier images, so a cycle costs one pass over the grown source set and never a pretraining run from scratch.

After training on $D^{(c)}$ we embed each image, $z_i^{(c)}=f_{\theta^{(c)}}(x_i)$, normalize to unit length, and compute
\begin{equation}
d_i^{(c)}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{z\in\mathcal N_k(z_i^{(c)};\,Z^{(c)}\setminus\{z_i^{(c)}\})}\lVert z_i^{(c)}-z\rVert_2^2,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal N_k(z;Z)$ is the set of $k$ nearest neighbors of $z$ in $Z$, so a large score marks an image in a sparse part of the current representation. We use $k=100$, and $k$ between 10 and 200 moves the seven-task average by about one point (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:selector-robustness-full-linear}).

\subsection{Sparse and recoverable anchor selection}
\label{sec:mode-window}
\begin{wrapfigure}{r}{0.44\textwidth}
\vspace{-8pt}
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.44\textwidth]{figures/mode_window_selection.pdf}
\vspace{-6pt}
\caption{The score distribution of one ImageNet-100-LT run. The mode window centers the candidate pool on the modal bin of the 75th to 99th percentile band (green). The top-tail control spends the same budget in the shaded extreme tail.}
\label{fig:mode-window}
\vspace{-6pt}
\end{wrapfigure}
The extreme tail is populated by artifacts, degenerate crops and images the generator cannot vary faithfully, and uniform sampling spends most of its budget in regions that are already dense. The selector targets a sparse region that is still internally coherent in three steps (Figure~\ref{fig:mode-window}). It restricts attention to $U^{(c)}=\{i\mid q^{(c)}_{0.75}\leq d_i^{(c)}\leq q^{(c)}_{0.99}\}$ and builds a histogram over that band whose modal bin has center $m^{(c)}$. Given a candidate-pool multiplier $\alpha$, it forms the mode-centered pool $C^{(c)}\subseteq U^{(c)}$ of size $\alpha B$ that minimizes $\sum_{i\in C}|d_i^{(c)}-m^{(c)}|$. It then selects the anchor set $S^{(c)}$ of size $B$ by greedy farthest-point sampling in embedding space from that pool. The band excludes the scores where faithful repair is least reliable, the mode favors a region with repeated empirical support over isolated endpoints, and farthest-point sampling keeps the budget off near-duplicates. We use $\alpha=4$ and $B=500$.

\subsection{Local generative repair and continued pretraining}
For each anchor we generate $G=5$ variants with a frozen image-to-image diffusion model $g_\phi$, conditioning on the anchor image under an empty text prompt so that the repair signal comes from the image and never from a class name, and set $D^{(c+1)}=D^{(c)}\cup\{g_\phi(x_i,\epsilon_j)\mid i\in S^{(c)},\,j\in[G]\}$. The noise $\epsilon_j$ indexes independent translations of one anchor, so variants keep coarse structure while changing texture, pose, color or composition. The main configuration is Stable Diffusion 3 Medium with 20 denoising steps, strength 0.6 and output resolution equal to the training crop. Five training stages contain four repairs, so a full run adds 10{,}000 images to a source of about 10{,}000. The score estimates support deficit, the mode window enforces recoverability, farthest-point sampling approximates coverage and cyclic re-estimation reallocates the budget after every intervention, the four requirements of Section~\ref{sec:theory}. Algorithm~\ref{alg:bridge} in the appendix gives the loop.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Sources, targets and protocol.}
We pretrain on five unlabeled source regimes, the long-tailed ImageNet-100-LT~\citep{imagenet-lt}, CIFAR-10-LT and CIFAR-100-LT~\citep{krizhevsky2009learning}, and 10k subsets of the web corpora PASS~\citep{pass} and DiffusionDB~\citep{diffusiondb}. Transfer is measured on seven benchmarks, CIFAR-10-LT, CIFAR-100-LT, Stanford Cars~\citep{cars}, FGVC Aircraft~\citep{aircraft}, Oxford Flowers~\citep{flowers}, Oxford-IIIT Pets~\citep{pets} and ImageNet-100-LT, by linear probe and by $k$NN classification on frozen features. Every number is a three-seed mean and standard deviation, and paired comparisons share seeds and source checkpoints. ResNet-50 is the default backbone because it is common to the baselines, and \method\ trains five cycles of 100 epochs with $k=100$, $B=500$ and $G=5$. DINO and MAE follow their published ViT recipes and are evaluated on ViT-S in paired experiments. Appendix~\ref{app:implementation} gives implementation details.

\paragraph{Compared methods and controls.}
The source-regime comparison covers source-matched SimCLR, TS, SDCLR and \method, with hybrids in the appendix. All ablations use ImageNet-100-LT source pretraining, the same downstream suite and three seeds, and vary one component at a time under the full five-cycle protocol. The generator ablation includes an oracle that returns the exact real images withheld when the long tail was constructed. This oracle is not a competing method, since it requires knowing which images are missing, information no label-free method has, and places true support at the true deficit. It is the ceiling for any repair operator at this budget, and a generator that reaches it recovers the full value of the withheld data without access to it. The single-stage controls of Section~\ref{sec:one-stage} branch from one source checkpoint, add the same number of images and receive the same optimizer updates.

Under the frozen cycle-0 encoder (Figure~\ref{fig:fixed-support}), inserting the generated images reduces the $k$NN radius of the treated anchors by 4.7 percent against 2.4 percent for controls matched one-to-one within class on initial radius, a paired difference of $-2.3$ points that is negative on every seed (Proposition~\ref{prop:local-support}, Appendix Table~\ref{tab:fixed-support}). Neighborhood label purity falls by 0.6 points at the anchors and by 2.0 at the controls, so the variants enter the anchors' neighborhoods as same-class support. All 500 anchors chosen at cycle 0 have radii in the 75th to 99th percentile band, and Figure~\ref{fig:cycle-histograms-main} shows why the band must be re-derived every cycle, since the score distribution keeps its shape while its scale grows. Global statistics of the learned space are objective-dependent (Appendix~\ref{app:geometry}).

\vspace{-6pt}
\section{Limitations}
\method\ depends on informative encoder neighborhoods in the first cycle and on faithful local generation, and its frozen generator imports the coverage and biases of its own pretraining data. The score band, neighborhood size and budget are defaults that transferred across five sources without tuning, and no optimum has been shown. The fixed-encoder test verifies where support is inserted without establishing how every learned neighborhood evolves, later cycles may select generated anchors, and no generated-image filter is applied. In our unoptimized setting the loop raises wall-clock time over the no-repair run by 29 to 46 percent, almost all of it diffusion sampling at the default 20 steps without caching (Appendix~\ref{app:compute}). A distilled few-step sampler, cached variants or generation on separate hardware would reduce that term directly, and since the generation budget is fixed while the pretraining cost grows with the source, the relative overhead falls with scale. Whether the fixed budget remains effective when the 10{,}000 added images are one percent of the source instead of 100 percent remains open.

\vspace{-6pt}
\section{Conclusion}
Skewed-data SSL can be treated as a feedback problem in which the learner allocates a finite budget to change its own future source distribution, and under diminishing returns to local support and fidelity that decays in the extreme tail, the budget belongs in a sparse interior band and should be reallocated after each repair. \method\ implements that policy without labels, improves transfer over source-matched baselines on five sources and four objectives, matches an oracle that restores the withheld real images, and adds support where its encoder said it was missing. Where the budget is spent has no measurable effect for one intervention and an effect of 8.0 points once the representation that chooses the target has itself been repaired.

\section{Proofs for the Support-Allocation Framework}
\label{app:proofs}

\begin{proposition}[Closed-form interior optimum]
\label{prop:closed-form}
Let $b(d)=b_0d^{\beta}$ with $b_0>0$ and $\beta\in(0,1]$, and let $\rho(d)=\rho_0e^{-\gamma d}$ with $\gamma>0$. Then $g$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,\infty)$ for every $\lambda\geq0$, so Proposition~\ref{prop:interior} applies whenever $g$ changes sign on the interval. If $\lambda=0$ the maximizer is $d^{\star}=\beta/\gamma$, independent of $b_0$ and $\rho_0$.
\end{proposition}

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:local-support}.}
Sort the distances from $z$ to points in $Z$ and denote their $k$th order statistic by $r_k(z;Z)$. Adding $A$ preserves every old candidate distance and may introduce smaller ones. The $k$th order statistic can therefore only decrease. It decreases strictly when an added distance enters the first $k$ positions and displaces a strictly larger distance. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:sparse-value}.}
Let $x=n_j+m_j+\kappa$. Since $f(x)=x^{-\gamma}$ is positive, strictly decreasing and strictly convex for $x>0$, the forward difference $f(x)-f(x+1)$ is positive and strictly decreases with $x$. Multiplication by $p_ja_j>0$ preserves both properties. Equal $p_ja_j$ therefore leaves effective support as the sole determinant of the marginal ordering. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:interior}.}
Rewrite the utility as $u(d)=\rho(d)(b(d)+\lambda)-\lambda$. Its derivative is
\[
u'(d)=\rho(d)(b(d)+\lambda)\left[\frac{b'(d)}{b(d)+\lambda}+\frac{\rho'(d)}{\rho(d)}\right]=\rho(d)(b(d)+\lambda)\,g(d).
\]
The factor preceding $g(d)$ is positive. Since $g$ is strictly decreasing and changes sign across the interval, it has exactly one root $d^\star$ by continuity. Hence $u$ increases before $d^\star$ and decreases after it, making $d^\star$ the unique global maximizer. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:closed-form}.}
With $b(d)=b_0d^{\beta}$ and $\rho(d)=\rho_0e^{-\gamma d}$ we have $\rho'(d)/\rho(d)=-\gamma$, so
\[
g(d)=\frac{b_0\beta d^{\beta-1}}{b_0d^{\beta}+\lambda}-\gamma=\frac{\beta d^{\beta-1}}{d^{\beta}+c}-\gamma,\qquad c=\lambda/b_0\geq0.
\]
Write $h(d)=\beta d^{\beta-1}(d^{\beta}+c)^{-1}$. Differentiating,
\[
h'(d)=\frac{\beta d^{\beta-2}\bigl[(\beta-1)(d^{\beta}+c)-\beta d^{\beta}\bigr]}{(d^{\beta}+c)^{2}}=\frac{\beta d^{\beta-2}\bigl[(\beta-1)c-d^{\beta}\bigr]}{(d^{\beta}+c)^{2}}.
\]
For $\beta\in(0,1]$ we have $(\beta-1)c\leq0<d^{\beta}$ for all $d>0$, so the bracket is strictly negative and $h'(d)<0$. Hence $g=h-\gamma$ is strictly decreasing for every $\lambda\geq0$. When $\lambda=0$ we have $c=0$ and $g(d)=\beta/d-\gamma$, whose unique root is $d^{\star}=\beta/\gamma$. Since $g$ is positive below it and negative above it, $d^{\star}$ is the maximizer by the argument of Proposition~\ref{prop:interior}. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:adaptive}.}
For region $j$ define the marginal sequence $\delta_{j,t}=U_j(n_j+t)-U_j(n_j+t-1)$ for $t\geq1$. Discrete concavity makes each sequence nonincreasing. Any feasible allocation of $B$ repairs selects a prefix of length $m_j$ from each sequence. Greedy allocation repeatedly chooses the largest available next element, and after $B$ steps it has selected the $B$ largest prefix-feasible marginals. If another allocation had larger utility, it would contain a selected marginal smaller than an available unselected marginal, and exchanging them would improve that allocation, which contradicts its optimality. A frozen allocation can fail this rule because it preserves the initial ordering after selected regions have moved to smaller marginals. \qed

\paragraph{Proof of Proposition~\ref{prop:one-stage}.}
Write $f(x)=x^{-\gamma}$. For $x>0$ the mean value theorem gives $f(x)-f(x+1)=\gamma\xi^{-\gamma-1}$ for some $\xi\in(x,x+1)$, so
\[
0<\Delta_j(m_j)=p_ja_j\bigl[f(n_j+m_j+\kappa)-f(n_j+m_j+1+\kappa)\bigr]\leq p_ja_j\gamma(n_j+\kappa)^{-\gamma-1},
\]
using $\xi>n_j+m_j+\kappa\geq n_j+\kappa$ and $m_j\geq0$. Both $\mathcal B(\mathbf m)$ and $\mathcal B(\mathbf m')$ are obtained from the common baseline $\mathcal B(\mathbf 0)$ by subtracting sums of at most $B$ such marginals, since $\sum_jm_j=\sum_jm_j'=B$. Each sum lies in $[0,\,B\gamma\max_jp_ja_j(n_j+\kappa)^{-\gamma-1}]$, and the difference of two numbers drawn from an interval is bounded by its length. Monotonicity of $(n+\kappa)^{-\gamma-1}$ in $n$ gives the $n_{\min}$ form. \qed

\section{Implementation and Reproducibility}
\label{app:implementation}

\paragraph{Training schedule.} The encoder is warm-started from the previous stage at every cycle and is never retrained from scratch. Each stage uses a fresh optimizer and learning-rate schedule. All earlier real and generated images remain in the source set, so the setting differs from sequential-task learning in which previous data disappear. Five training stages contain four repairs, since generation runs only when another stage follows. The cycle ablation holds the total optimizer update budget and the total number of generated images fixed and compares fewer repairs with more learning between them against more frequent repairs with less.

\paragraph{Core configuration.}
The main experiments use ResNet-50 encoders, five cycles of 100 epochs, mean $k$NN distance with $k=100$, 500 anchors per cycle and five variants per anchor. The optimizer is AdamW with weight decay $10^{-4}$, and method-specific learning rates and temperatures follow the settings of each baseline. The paired ViT-S experiments use the reference recipe of each objective. DINO uses two global and eight local crops at $96\times96$ with a bottleneck projection head, and MAE runs five cycles of 80 epochs.

\paragraph{Scoring and selection.}
Scoring uses normalized $\ell_2$ distance, and raw $\ell_2$, cosine and multiscale-$k$ variants are reported as a robustness study in Table~\ref{tab:selector-robustness-full-linear}. The mode-window selector restricts the score distribution to the 75th to 99th percentile range, builds a histogram with the automatic bin heuristic, forms a mode-centered candidate pool of size $4B$ and applies greedy farthest-point sampling on normalized features to select the final $B$ anchors.

\paragraph{Generators.}
The main method uses Stable Diffusion 3 Medium in image-to-image mode. With an empty prompt the guidance scale is inert, since at a fixed seed scales 3.0, 5.0 and 7.5 produce bit-identical images and scale 1.0 differs by a mean absolute pixel error of $0.0016$ at 49.7 dB. The generation ablation replaces it with FLUX or with the oracle, which adds the withheld real images and runs no generator. In the PASS and DiffusionDB experiments the SD3 batch size is 12 to fit the 24-hour wall-time limit.

\paragraph{Single-stage controls.} Every arm of Table~\ref{tab:closest-prior-empirical} branches from one SimCLR ResNet-50 checkpoint per seed, adds 2{,}500 images where it generates, and receives the same number of optimizer updates. The TADA-style arm ranks source images by the decrease of their SSL loss over the first stage and selects the slowest-learned 500. The inverse cluster-frequency arm clusters the current embeddings and samples anchors with probability inverse to cluster occupancy. The conventional-augmentation arm applies the standard SimCLR augmentation pipeline five times to each mode-window anchor in place of the generator. The DiffAug arm trains a conditional diffusion generator jointly with the encoder on the source, following the published objective, and its \method\ variant conditions that generator on the mode-window anchors. The distillation arm adds a feature-matching loss between the encoder and frozen SD3 activations on the source images and adds no image.

\paragraph{Source data.}
PASS and DiffusionDB use \texttt{train[:10000]} with pretraining split override \texttt{[1.0, 0.0, 0.0]}. The long-tailed ImageNet-100, CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100 sources use their standard long-tailed configurations without the 10k restriction.

\paragraph{Seeds and reporting.}
All claims rest on three-seed averages reported as mean and standard deviation. Paired comparisons share seeds and, where stated, source checkpoints. Multiple comparisons within one table are corrected by the Holm procedure. Before publication we verify that no two conditions in a table share an encoder fingerprint.

\paragraph{Code availability.}
An anonymous code release is available at \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/status/FOMO-0488}.

\section{Compute Resources and Runtime}
\label{app:compute}
Most experiments use one node with two NVIDIA A100 80GB GPUs under a 24-hour wall-time limit. The DINO arm fits on a single 48GB card at the batch size used. The 454 completed runs retained for this paper total approximately 5{,}471 wall-clock hours, of which the baselines and controls that appear only in the appendix account for 2{,}987. Runs on two-GPU nodes consume roughly twice that in GPU hours, while the paired ViT-S runs hold a single card each. These totals exclude failed runs, timeouts, cache migration and pilot jobs, so the project consumed more compute than the successful totals alone.

Representative mean wall-clock times over three seeds are as follows. On ImageNet-100-LT, SimCLR takes 16.9 hours and \method\ 21.8. On PASS, 10.2 and 14.9. On DiffusionDB, 11.6 and 16.4. The loop therefore raises wall-clock time by 29 to 46 percent over the corresponding no-repair run, of which the embedding pass is a small fraction and diffusion sampling the remainder. Four repairs add 10{,}000 images in total, which is 100 percent of a 10k source, 10 percent of a 100k source and 1 percent of a one-million-image source. This describes the relative cost under a fixed budget and does not establish that the fixed budget remains effective at those scales.

\section{Algorithm}
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\method\ training loop with mode-window selection}
\label{alg:bridge}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\STATE Initialize source set $D^{(0)}$, encoder $f_{\theta^{(0)}}$, frozen generator $g_{\phi}$, stage lengths $\{E_c\}_{c=0}^{C-1}$
\FOR{$c = 0, \dots, C-1$}
    \STATE Continue training $f_{\theta^{(c)}}$ on $D^{(c)}$ for $E_c$ epochs with a fresh optimizer schedule
    \IF{$c<C-1$}
        \STATE Compute normalized embeddings $Z^{(c)} = \{f_{\theta^{(c)}}(x_i) \mid x_i \in D^{(c)}\}$
        \STATE Compute mean $k$NN scores $\{d_i^{(c)}\}$ from $Z^{(c)}$
        \STATE Restrict to the 75th to 99th percentile band and locate the modal bin center $m^{(c)}$
        \STATE Form the candidate pool $C^{(c)}$ of size $\alpha B$ whose scores are closest to $m^{(c)}$
        \STATE Select $S^{(c)} \subseteq C^{(c)}$ with $|S^{(c)}| = B$ by greedy farthest-point sampling in embedding space
        \STATE Form $\widetilde{D}^{(c)} = \{g_{\phi}(x_i, \epsilon_j) \mid i \in S^{(c)},\, j \in [G]\}$ and set $D^{(c+1)} = D^{(c)} \cup \widetilde{D}^{(c)}$
    \ENDIF
\ENDFOR
\STATE Return the final encoder $f_{\theta^{(C-1)}}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
